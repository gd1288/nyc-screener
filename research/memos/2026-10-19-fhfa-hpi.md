# FHFA House Price Index (HPI) — research memo

**Date:** 2026-10-19 · **Candidate file:** `research/candidates/fhfa-hpi.json` · **Recommendation: recommend**

## What it is
A repeat-sales house price index published quarterly by the Federal Housing Finance Agency,
covering all 50 states and 400+ cities/MSAs, built from Fannie Mae/Freddie Mac mortgage records.
Free bulk CSV/JSON/XML/SQL downloads from fhfa.gov/data/hpi/datasets, no login, no key, no
commercial-use restriction found.

## Why it could help
Important correction from an earlier draft of this memo: the project **does** already compute a
repeat-sales appreciation signal — `app/sources/dof_sales.py`'s `repeat_sales_cagr()` pairs up
units that sold twice (>=2 years apart) from NYC Dept. of Finance condo sales and writes
`condo_repeat_sales_cagr` per NTA as a `neighborhood_metric`. So the gap here is **not** "no
transaction-based signal exists."

The real gap is **coverage and independence**:

1. **NYC-DOF-only, and sparse.** `dof_sales.py` filters on NYC-specific `building_class_category`
   values and pulls from `data.cityofnewyork.us` — it has no equivalent anywhere else in the US,
   and even within NYC it needs >=15 qualifying sale pairs per NTA to report at all
   (`MIN_SALES_FOR_STAT`). The moment the project points outside NYC — its own stated goal — this
   signal disappears entirely. FHFA HPI gives the same *kind* of transaction-based evidence
   nationally, at MSA grain, with no city-specific scraping required.
2. **The backtest has no independent check.** `scoring/backtest.py` validates the growth score's
   price pillars only against Zillow ZHVI history (`hist_zhvi_%` metrics) — not against the NYC
   DOF repeat-sales CAGR, and not against anything FHFA. The research-sources skill
   (`.claude/skills/research-sources/SKILL.md`) names "FHFA tract HPI growth" as part of the
   evaluation benchmark it expects candidate series to be checked against, yet a direct
   `grep -rni fhfa backend/` returns **zero hits** — it's referenced as a yardstick but never
   actually ingested.

## Evaluation
- **Geography:** state and MSA (including a dedicated top-100-MSA tool), plus 3-/5-digit ZIP
  (explicitly labeled "developmental," purchase-only index). **No census-tract-level series was
  found in this run** — which conflicts with the skill's own "FHFA tract HPI growth" phrasing.
  Treat that as imprecise skill wording rather than fact; MSA appears to be the finest
  non-developmental grain FHFA actually publishes. Re-verify directly against
  fhfa.gov/data/hpi/datasets before building on either description.
- **History:** US/census-division/state series from 1975Q1; MSA all-transactions start dates vary
  by metro (several from the mid-1990s); purchase-only generally from 1991Q1.
- **Cost/license:** free, no key, no rate limit (static bulk file). No commercial-use restriction
  found — standard federal statistical output.
- **Quantitative correlation:** not run. `app/research/evaluate_series.py` (Phase 4) doesn't exist
  in this checkout yet, so this evaluation is qualitative only, as the skill's step 4 allows when
  that tool isn't built.

## Caveats / what to re-check before implementing
- Could not WebFetch fhfa.gov directly this run (sandbox DNS/proxy issue to that host) — the file
  layout, exact ZIP-level file naming, and the tract-vs-MSA granularity question above should all
  be re-confirmed against the live data dictionary before writing an adapter.
- It's a bulk static file, not a Socrata endpoint — don't reuse `app/sources/socrata.py`'s client
  for it.

## Recommendation
**Recommend.** Highest priority of the five candidates in this run. It's free, truly national in
a way the project's existing NYC-only repeat-sales signal is not, and it would let the backtest
validate against a series independent of Zillow's estimate methodology instead of relying on
ZHVI alone. Suggested shape: a `neighborhood`-kind source pulling the quarterly MSA
all-transactions series, written to `neighborhood_metrics` alongside (not replacing)
`condo_repeat_sales_cagr` and the Zillow Research series.
