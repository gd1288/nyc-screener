# BLS Local Area Unemployment Statistics (LAUS) — research memo

**Date:** 2026-10-19 · **Candidate file:** `research/candidates/bls-laus.json` · **Recommendation: lean-recommend**

## What it is
BLS's monthly unemployment-rate and labor-force series for ~7,600 areas (counties, cities, metro
areas/divisions, states), produced via the "Handbook method" reconciling local estimates to
statewide totals. Public domain, free. Bulk flat files and the Series Report tool need no login;
the full Public Data API (v2) supports a free optional registration key for higher query limits.

## Why it could help
The neighborhood growth pillar (`scoring/neighborhood.py`) currently draws on development/rezoning
activity, transit access, planned catalysts, crime, and Census ACS demographics — but nothing
captures the local labor market directly. Employment growth and unemployment rate are standard
leading indicators for housing demand and aren't represented anywhere in the current metric set.

## Evaluation
- **Geography:** county, metro area/division, state, place — nationwide.
- **Update frequency:** monthly, with annual-average revisions.
- **History:** generally extends back to the 1990s or earlier depending on area.
- **Cost/license:** free, public domain. Unregistered API access has a lower daily query limit
  than a registered free key; exact numbers not independently confirmed this run (see caveat).

## Caveats / what to re-check before implementing
- Could not reach bls.gov directly this run (sandbox DNS/proxy issue) — confirm the registered vs.
  unregistered query limits and current API terms against bls.gov/developers before implementing.
- No quantitative correlation against FHFA HPI/Zillow ZORI was computed (`evaluate_series.py`
  doesn't exist in this checkout yet) — unemployment/employment growth as a housing-appreciation
  leading indicator is a reasonable prior from general real-estate research, not something
  verified against this project's own markets this run.

## Recommendation
**Lean-recommend.** A legitimate, free, genuinely national gap-filler for the growth pillar, but
the weakest of this run's five candidates: it adds one more general-economy signal alongside
several the project already has, rather than fixing a concrete hardcoded assumption (FRED) or a
missing pillar entirely (FEMA NRI, HUD FMR for non-NYC rent) or the named-but-unbuilt backtest
ground truth (FHFA HPI). Worth a future look if the growth pillar needs more signal, but lower
priority than the other four candidates in this run.
