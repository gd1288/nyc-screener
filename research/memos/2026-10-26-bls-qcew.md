# BLS Quarterly Census of Employment and Wages (QCEW)

**Date:** 2026-10-26 · **Candidate file:** `research/candidates/bls-qcew.json`

## What it is
BLS's county-level employment and average-weekly-wage series by industry, covering over 95% of U.S.
jobs reported by employers. Free bulk CSV/ZIP downloads with no login; a separate BLS Public Data API
(v2.0) offers a free registration key with higher daily query limits (500 vs 25 unregistered), though
it's unconfirmed whether QCEW specifically is served through that keyed API or only the bulk files.
Released roughly 6 months after quarter-end.

## Why it could help
No current source in this app touches local employment or wage growth. The closest existing pillar,
`demographics` (`app/sources/census_acs.py`), covers income *level* and 10-year change from ACS,
which is a slower-moving, 5-year-rolling-average measure. QCEW would add a genuinely different,
higher-frequency jobs signal — a classic demand-side driver of housing demand that nothing in the
current pillar set captures directly.

## Granularity problem, stated plainly
QCEW's finest usable geography is **county**. NYC's five boroughs are each their own county, so this
app's 262 NTAs would collapse to 5 distinct values — it cannot differentiate neighborhoods within a
borough at all. That's a real limitation for the current NYC-NTA screener, not a minor caveat.

## Verification caveat
bls.gov / data.bls.gov returned `403 connect_rejected` from this session's proxy on every attempt
(the same session-wide block that hit all five candidates). Findings are from WebSearch summaries of

## Recommendation: **Lean recommend** (sequence it with the area-model migration, not now)
Free, government, long history (1990s+), and directly in-scope per this run's instructions. But its
real payoff only shows up once the project covers multiple metros (per CLAUDE.md's "generalizing to
any US neighborhood" goal) and scoring runs per-CBSA rather than NYC-only (`backend/CLAUDE.md`'s
described area-model migration) — county is a normal, useful granularity for most of the country.
Inside today's NYC-only, NTA-percentile screener it adds little. Worth implementing as part of that
migration, not as a standalone add right now.
