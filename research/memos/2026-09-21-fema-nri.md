# FEMA National Risk Index (NRI)

**Candidate file:** `research/candidates/fema-nri.json`
**Verdict: recommend_with_caveat**

## What it is
FEMA's composite natural-hazard risk score, covering 18 hazard types (flood, wildfire, hurricane,
heat wave, winter weather, earthquake, and more) for every US county and census tract. Distributed
as CSV/Shapefile/File-Geodatabase on OpenFEMA (https://www.fema.gov/about/openfema/data-sets/
national-risk-index-data), no login. It's a point-in-time composite, not a time series — FEMA
issues periodic full-dataset revisions rather than continuous updates.

## Why it could help
`livability.py`'s `FutureFloodplain` source is explicitly NYC-only: it pulls a single NYC Open
Data GeoJSON layer (`27ya-gqtm`, 2050s floodplain projection) and computes area-share overlap with
NYC NTAs. That's a good NYC-specific signal, but it's flood-only and geographically nothing else.
NRI would be the natural national successor/complement — same "climate risk penalty" role in
`neighborhood.py`'s scoring, but with 18 hazard categories and coverage for every US county/tract.

## Evaluation
- **Coverage/granularity:** national, county and tract level — every US county, unlike the
  NYC-only current source.
- **Cost/license:** free, no login, public-domain federal data, plain CSV downloads. Clean.
- **History:** not applicable — it's a current-snapshot composite risk score, not a growth series,
  so there's no "history available for backtest" question here the way there is for the price/rent
  candidates.
- **Correlation:** not applicable in the usual sense either. NRI isn't a growth/appreciation
  predictor to correlate against HPI/ZORI; its role is a risk penalty like the existing flood
  metric, so its evaluation criterion is completeness of coverage, not predictive power.

## Recommendation
Recommend with a scoping caveat, not a straight approve: for NYC condos specifically, NRI is
redundant with the existing, more NYC-tailored `future_floodplain_pct` (that source already covers
the one hazard — flooding — that dominates NYC climate risk, at a finer NYC-specific projection).
NRI's real value is as the national fallback once the project actually covers neighborhoods outside
NYC, at which point `future_floodplain_pct` has literally nothing to say. There's no urgency to
implement this before that geographic expansion happens; flagging it now so it's evaluated and on
record rather than rediscovered later, per the standing "read the gap" step of this procedure.
