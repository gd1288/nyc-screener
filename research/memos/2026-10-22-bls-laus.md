# BLS Local Area Unemployment Statistics (LAUS)

**Recommendation: conditional**

## What it is
BLS's monthly employment/unemployment estimates for ~7,600 areas, including every US county
(series IDs like `LAUCN360610000000004` for New York County). Free via the BLS Public Data API;
v1 works unauthenticated at low limits, v2 needs a free registration key for higher daily limits.
No paid tier.

## Why it could help
`backend/app/scoring/neighborhood.py` (Growth Score) currently draws on development/rezoning
activity, transit access, demographics (`census_acs.py`), and livability factors — but nothing
about the local job market. Local employment growth and unemployment trends are a standard,
well-studied leading indicator of housing demand, and BLS LAUS is the free, no-ambiguity
government source for it.

## Evaluation
- **Granularity**: county. This is the catch: the screener's unit of analysis is the NTA
  (neighborhood tabulation area), well below county. Every neighborhood in, say, Brooklyn would
  get the same Kings County unemployment number. That's a real loss of resolution, though no
  finer-grained free monthly employment series exists — `census_acs.py`'s ACS data has some
  employment-status fields but at annual-release cadence, not a dedicated monthly time series.
- **Cost/license**: free; v2 key removes most practical rate-limit concerns; public-domain data.
- **History**: multi-decade for most counties; BLS API v2 allows up to 20 years per request.
- **Quantitative evaluation**: not run (evaluation tooling doesn't exist yet). This is exactly the
  kind of candidate that tooling is for — "sounds useful" is not the same as "shows measurable
  lead/lag against appreciation," and county-level employment is a plausible but unproven input.

## Recommendation
Conditional recommend. Worth adding as a borough/county-level macro overlay (same resolution
tradeoff a FRED national rate series would have), but I'd hold off treating it as a scored Growth
Score input until it can actually be checked for correlation/lead-lag against FHFA HPI or Zillow
ZORI — don't want to add a plausible-sounding factor that turns out to carry no signal once
measured.
